How a coin makes the cut

Two detectors have to agree.

Three detectors look at every coin after the close. One firing is activity, not a setup. A coin goes further only when at least two of them agree.

2x THE 30-DAY MEDIAN

Volume expansion

Yesterday's dollar volume was more than twice its 30-day median, on an up day. Fresh participation, not drift.

CLOSE ABOVE THE 20-DAY HIGH

Range breakout

The daily close cleared the highest high of the prior 20 days. A trend continuation attempt.

+5 POINTS VS BTC OVER 7 DAYS

Strength vs BTC

The coin's 7-day return beat Bitcoin's by at least five percentage points. Money rotating in, not the whole market lifting.

  1. Two detectors agree. One alone goes to The Cut.
  2. Enough volume to trade. At least $2M traded on the day.
  3. The stop can survive noise. It sits at the 10-day low, at least one day's average range away and no more than 15% away.
  4. The target is a level we found. The highest high of the last 120 days, above the entry. No round-number guesses.
  5. The reward covers the risk. At least 2x the distance to the stop.
  6. Not on cooldown. A coin published in the last 7 days waits.
The record

No numbers until they mean something.

Every published card is followed until it hits its stop, its target, or its 10-day time stop. The coins we passed on are followed the same way, so the two can be compared. When a single day touches both stop and target, the order is unknowable from daily data, so it is marked ambiguous rather than counted as a win. Averages publish once 30 cards have resolved.
